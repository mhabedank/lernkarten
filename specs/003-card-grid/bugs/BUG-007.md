# Bug Report: The A8 card is portrait, and a flashcard is landscape

**Type**: Spec gap
**Severity**: High — the feature's headline output is the wrong shape for its
purpose. Nothing crashes, and A7 (the default) is untouched, so no existing
project is affected; but every card `--grid a8` has ever produced is rotated
90° from how a flashcard is read
**Feature**: 003-card-grid
**Reported**: 2026-08-20
**Status**: Reported
**Shipped in**: v0.4.0, v0.4.1

## Description

`--grid a8` produces a **portrait** card, 52.5 × 74.25 mm — taller than it is
wide. Flashcards are landscape: A7 today is 105 × 74.25 mm, and the boxes and
ready-made cards this feature was justified against are sold *quer* (landscape).
The reporter's words: *"the cards are read vertically, but learning cards are
horizontal — never seen it in another way."*

The cause is arithmetic, and it is the defining property of the A-series:
**every halving flips the orientation.**

| Grid | Card at `--margin 0` | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| `2x4` on A4 portrait | 105.00 × 74.25 mm | landscape ✅ |
| `4x4` on A4 portrait | **52.50 × 74.25 mm** | **portrait ❌** |

Halving a landscape card's width does not give a smaller landscape card. It
gives a portrait one. A7 landscape is 105 × 74; A8 landscape is 74 × 52 — not
52 × 74.

### The second half of the report: the bands

The reporter also notes the meta information is cut. That is a real and
separate consequence, and it gets **worse**, not better, once the orientation
is fixed. `head-h` (8.6 mm) and `foot-h` (6.2 mm) are absolute:

| | Card at `--margin 5` | Bands as % of height | Head-band label box | Writing area vs A7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A7 | 100 × 71.75 landscape | 20.6 % | 84.6 mm (~53 ch) | 100 % |
| A8 as shipped | 50 × 71.75 **portrait** | 20.6 % | 34.6 mm (~22 ch) | 50.0 % |
| A8 corrected | 71.75 × 50 landscape | **29.6 %** | **56.4 mm (~35 ch)** | **44.3 %** |

So fixing the orientation *widens* the head band — the ~22-character budget
becomes ~35 — but costs height: the fixed bands eat nearly 30 % of the card,
and the writing area drops below the portrait version's. **The label-clipping
problem and the field-space problem trade against each other, and the fix
changes which one binds.** Every measured constant this feature introduced was
measured against the wrong rectangle.

## Root Cause Analysis

**The spec recorded the orientation flip and never treated it as a question.**

`spec.md:32` — in the research table that chose this grid — reads:

| Grid | Card at `--margin 0` | A-series | Per sheet | Box you can buy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2 × 4** | 105 × 74.25 mm | **A7 landscape** | 8 | yes — today's card |
| **4 × 4** | 52.5 × 74.25 mm | **A8 portrait** | 16 | yes — A8 Lernbox |

The word **portrait** is right there, in bold, in the column that says
*landscape* one row above. The research went as far as citing
"HAN A7 **quer** Karteikarten" — *quer* is German for landscape — as evidence
that the size is standard, and did not notice it was evidence about the
orientation too. The selection criterion was "is there a box you can buy", and a
portrait A8 card does fit an A8 box, so the row passed.

Every later stage inherited the answer rather than re-deriving it. Analyze,
the cross-model review, the implementation-readiness checklist and the
post-implementation verification all had the number in front of them; none
asked whether a portrait flashcard is a flashcard. The phrase "the same card cut
down the middle, same height, half the width" then travelled verbatim from the
spec into `docs/design.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and the constitution — a description of
the defect, repeated as a design rule.

**SC-007, the manual print gate, would probably have caught it** — you cannot
hold a portrait card in your hand and still call it a flashcard. It is the one
check that was deferred, which is why this reached two releases.

## Recommended Fix

The geometry is unambiguous: **turn the sheet for A8.**

```
A4 landscape (297 × 210) ÷ 4 columns = 74.25 mm  ┐
A4 landscape (297 × 210) ÷ 4 rows    = 52.50 mm  ┘  landscape A8, 16 up, exact
```

One decision is settled and one is open, both design judgements under
constitution XVI:

1. **Do `head-h` and `foot-h` stay absolute at A8?** *(open)* At 8.6 + 6.2 mm
   they take 29.6 % of a landscape A8 card against 20.6 % of an A7 one. This is
   the reporter's "meta information is cut", and it needs an answer rather than
   a measurement.

   | | Head / foot | Field height | Bands | Type |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | (a) leave absolute | 8.6 / 6.2 mm | 35.20 mm | 29.6 % | unchanged |
   | (b) grid-dependent | 6.0 / 4.3 mm at A8 | 39.69 mm | 20.6 % | unchanged |

   (b) buys 12.8 % more field height and costs a smaller side marker, which is
   a `head-h` square. Neither option touches type size, so the 11 pt floor
   holds either way. Whether (b) contradicts "three bands that never move"
   depends on what that phrase binds: if it means *never move as the content
   changes*, so the eye always finds the same information in the same place on
   a given card, then (b) is a clarification to XVI rather than an amendment —
   the brand render already draws the bands at 2 x without that counting as a
   violation.
2. ~~**Does `--grid` now imply page orientation**, or does orientation become
   its own concept?~~ **DECIDED 2026-08-20 by the maintainer: the card is
   always landscape.** That is now a requirement rather than an assumption —
   precisely the gap this bug reports. The sheet turns to make it true: A7
   tiles a portrait A4 at 2 x 4, A8 tiles a landscape A4 at 4 x 4, both
   exactly. Any future grid must satisfy the invariant or be refused, which
   settles A6 and A9 before they are asked for.

   `scale` is **not** the mechanism for any of this. It is a uniform zoom —
   `assets/brand/example-cards.typ` calls `faces(cw, ch, scale: 2.0)` with
   `cw`/`ch` doubled to draw the identical card at twice the size. A
   `scale < 1.0` would shrink 11 pt reading text below the constitution XVI
   floor, and FR-011 forbids it.

### Evidence: rendered at A8 landscape with real cards

`templates/card.typ` was rendered at 74.25 × 52.5 mm with two demo cards, at
`scale: 0.7071` and at `scale: 1.0`. The fit question is settled and it is not
close:

| | 11 pt kept (options a/b) | uniform scale, 7.78 pt |
|---|---|---|
| long `TOPIC / SUBTOPIC` label | **wraps to two lines** — the band was never meant to allow it | fits on one line |
| a 150-character back | **overflows the card edge** | fits, with both note rules |
| note rules | one survives, sometimes none | both survive |

At 11 pt on a 52.5 mm-tall card the design stops working. This also explains why
the shipped portrait version looked plausible: it kept the full 74.25 mm of
height and halved only the width, so the field never got short enough to break.

**A third option therefore does exist, and it is the one the reporter proposed:**

| | Head / foot | Reading text | Same deck prints at A8? |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) leave absolute | 8.6 / 6.2 mm | 11 pt | no — most cards overflow |
| (b) grid-dependent bands | 6.0 / 4.3 mm | 11 pt | no — ~160 characters |
| **(c) uniform scale 1/√2** | 6.08 / 4.38 mm | **7.78 pt** | **yes — identical card, half the paper** |

(c) is what SC-001 actually promised: "half the sheets for the same deck". Under
(a) or (b) an existing deck cannot be printed at A8 at all without rewriting its
cards, which makes the headline feature conditional on rewriting the content it
was supposed to reprint.

The cost of (c) is the type floor. `docs/design.md:45` sets 11 pt because
"Archivo has wider apertures than a true geometric sans, so it survives 11 pt on
cheap paper" — a claim about print, and the only one of these three questions a
screen cannot answer. A printable comparison at true size is the instrument;
this is SC-007 arriving early, on exactly the question it exists for.

If (c) is chosen, constitution XVI's floor has to be **scoped rather than
lowered** — it was already re-scoped once in 2.4.0 for the same reason on the
landing page. The scoping that fits the evidence: the floor binds the card at
its reference size, and a grid may render that card at an A-series scale.

2. `/speckit.bugfix.verify` — confirm consistency across the artifacts.
3. Re-open T036, T047, T048 and the docs sweep; re-measure the four constants

4. Amend the constitution again — XVI is wrong as ratified in 2.5.0.

6. Correct the v0.4.0 and v0.4.1 release notes, which state the wrong dimension
