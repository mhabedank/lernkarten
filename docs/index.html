the pipeline

Seven steps, two of them optional. Every step writes a plain text file you can read and edit, and every step is repeatable: add a source next month, run it again, and you only pay for what is new.

Aheader band

Topic and subtopic on the left. On the right a coloured square: a red circle on the front, a yellow disc on the back. Colour and shape and position — remove any two and the card still tells you which side you hold.

Bthe field

One prompt, flush left, centred in the band. The back drops to reading size and never runs to the edge — if it does, the build warns you and you split the card in two.

Cfooter band

The mark, then lernkarten by mhabedank, then the card id and 1/2 or 2/2, so a dropped stack can be rebuilt. The mark's box is hollow on the front and solid on the back — the one signal you need when eight cards land face-down.

Dnote space

Two dotted rules above the footer, wherever the answer leaves room, for the note you write the third time you get the card wrong. The one thing paper does that an app cannot.

one grid, any subject

Statistics, anatomy, French vocabulary, the parts of a diesel engine — the card does not care. One card is one fact, phrased as an active recall prompt, short enough to read in a queue.

Cards come out in the language of your sources. Hyphenation and quotation marks follow automatically, and files in different languages can go into the same PDF.

it is just yaml

topic: 'Probability'
language: english
cards:
  - subtopic: 'Bayes theorem'
    front: 'How is Bayes theorem stated?'
    back: '$P(A | B) = (P(B | A) P(A)) / P(B)$'
    source: 'Lecture 3, slide 12'

One file per topic. If a card is wrong, you fix a line and print again — nothing is a black box.

03

print it, cut it

Eight cards to an A4 page, fronts and backs on consecutive pages with the backs mirrored so duplex lines them up.

page 1 · fronts

12345678

page 2 · backs, mirrored

21436587
1

Duplex, flip on long edge

Short edge puts every back upside down — the one setting that ruins a whole stack.

2

100 % scale, not "fit to page"

Fit-to-page shrinks the sheet and the fronts stop meeting the backs.

3

Cut the long line first

One vertical cut down the middle, then three horizontal ones: eight cards, twelve seconds.

A 5 mm free margin is kept by default (cards 100 × 71.75 mm) so printers with a non-printable edge clip nothing, and crop marks in that margin show where to cut. Borderless printers get the full A7 with --margin 0.

your material never leaves

Sources, extracted texts and cards are plain files in your own folder. The pipeline uploads nothing, ever.

writing is the slow part

The machine writes the cards. Shuffling and reading them — the part that actually makes things stick — stays with you.

no toolchain to install

No document toolchain to set up, and nothing to install by hand. The typesetting engine downloads itself once on the first build.

04

install

Two lines in Claude Code. That is the whole installation.

/plugin marketplace add mhabedank/lernkarten
/plugin install lernkarten@mhabedank

Then go to the folder where you want your cards to live and walk the pipeline. The build also runs on its own, without Claude Code: clone the repository and call lernkarten build cards/*.yaml.
